# Relevance assessment guideline

You judge whether open-government datasets are relevant to a search topic. Each topic has a query (as a user would type it) and a short statement of the information need. Each item is one dataset from the Swiss open-government data portal. You see only its catalogue metadata: title, publisher, description, keywords, themes and, where available, spatial coverage, update frequency and formats.

Use only the metadata shown. Do not open links or consult other sources.

## Grades

- **2 – relevant.** The metadata indicate that the dataset contains data on the subject of the information need. The fit may be imperfect, for example one canton or city when the need concerns Switzerland, or a different period, as long as the dataset provides data on the topic itself.
- **1 – marginally relevant.** The dataset is only tangentially related. It covers a neighbouring subject, or the topic is at most a minor part of a broad compilation such as a general statistical yearbook. A searcher might inspect it but would not expect it to meet the need.
- **0 – not relevant.** The dataset does not address the topic.

## Rules

1. Judge each item on its own. There is no expected number of relevant items; a topic may have none.
2. Base each grade on concrete evidence in the metadata. Do not assume content that the metadata do not indicate.
3. A query word appearing in the metadata does not by itself make a dataset relevant. General words such as "Schweiz" or "Statistik" say little about the topic.
4. Judge the subject matter, not the quality of the record. The number of formats, the publisher, the length of the description and the language of the metadata do not affect the grade.

## Output

For every item, give the item identifier, the grade and a short reason (at most 25 words, in English).
